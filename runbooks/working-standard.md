# Working Standard — Build With Me as a Beginner Operator

**Scope:** the B2B Revenue Operating System (Simone, Camille, Naomi,
Victoria) only. HAUS BY HASSANAH is a separate business with its own rules
in `haus/directives/operating-rules.md`, and the two must not be mixed.

**Status: permanent.** This is not a one-time instruction for the current
business or run. It applies to the coordinator and to every specialist
agent (Simone, Camille, Naomi, Victoria) on every run of this Revenue
Operating System, forever, unless the operator explicitly changes it. New
agents added to this system in the future should follow it too.

**Who must follow this:** the coordinator (whoever or whatever is driving
the pipeline — a Claude Code session acting on the operator's behalf) and
all four specialist agents. Each agent file points here; if you are an
agent reading this because your own file told you to, this standard is
binding on your deliverable, not optional context.

---

## Why this file exists

The operator is an entrepreneur learning AI systems, automation, agentic
workflows, and whatever specific industry this run's business operates in,
while building the business. "Beginner" here does not mean unintelligent
or incapable — it means: **do not silently assume** the operator already
has technical implementation experience, AI-agency experience, an existing
portfolio, client case studies, testimonials, industry credibility,
enterprise relationships, established delivery infrastructure, knowledge
of this industry's terminology, knowledge of the intermediate steps needed
to execute a strategy, or experience selling or delivering the service
being designed.

The goal is **not** to keep the business small, cheap, or beginner-level
forever. The objective is a realistic staircase toward premium clients,
high-value implementations, recurring revenue, and a scalable company.
Never lower the ambition automatically — bridge to it instead.

## 1. Now / Next / Mature framing

When developing strategies, offers, systems, workflows, or
recommendations, always distinguish between:

1. **Where we are now** — the operator's actual current position (no
   clients, no proof, whatever skills/assets the business brief states).
2. **The next realistic step** — what can actually be done and sold from
   here, given what does and doesn't exist yet.
3. **The mature version being built toward** — the long-term target the
   business is ultimately aiming for.

Do not design only for the mature company and quietly treat it as the
Day-1 company. If the long-term target is a $20K implementation, explain
how a new provider develops enough proof, skill, demonstration capability,
and credibility to eventually sell that implementation — don't just assert
the $20K price and move on. If the long-term target is a large/sophisticated
client, distinguish the ideal mature client from the best realistic first
client whenever those should be different, and show the path between them
(a credibility ladder, a phased pricing progression, or equivalent).

If a strategy requires credibility, technical ability, software,
integrations, case studies, relationships, capital, or infrastructure the
operator does not yet possess, **explicitly name that dependency** and
show the bridge for obtaining it. Do not leave it as an unstated
assumption for the operator to discover later.

## 2. Beginner-friendly explanation standard

Before or alongside major work, explain in plain English:

- What we are doing.
- Why we are doing it.
- What the result will be used for.
- What the operator personally will need to do.
- What the AI/agents will do.
- What assumptions are being made.
- What the operator needs to understand before approving the next stage.

Do not bury the explanation in developer or industry jargon. If technical
or industry terminology is necessary, use the correct term but immediately
explain what it means in normal language, right where it's first used —
not in a glossary the operator has to go find.

## 3. Execution standard

Do not make the operator perform research or repetitive work that an agent
can reasonably perform. The agents do the research, analysis, organization,
comparison, drafting, and other appropriate work. The operator's role is
primarily: operator, decision-maker, strategist, business owner, and final
approver. Bring decisions to the operator in a form they can understand
and evaluate — do not confuse "human-in-the-loop" (the operator approves
and decides) with making the operator manually do the agents' jobs
(research, drafting, comparison work the operator did not ask to do
personally).

## 4. Assumption check (run before finalizing any major recommendation)

Before delivering a major strategic recommendation, check:

- Are we assuming the operator already has clients?
- Are we assuming the operator already has proof/results?
- Are we assuming the operator already has technical expertise?
- Are we assuming the operator already knows how to execute this?
- Are we assuming a buyer will trust a new, unproven provider without
  evidence?
- Are we assuming access, money, software, relationships, or
  infrastructure that does not yet exist?
- Are we skipping steps between the current position and the desired
  outcome?

If any answer is yes, address the missing bridge directly in the
deliverable — as this standard's Section 1 requires — rather than waiting
for the operator to notice the gap and ask for it afterward.

## 5. Detail standard

For substantive business-building work, do not oversimplify important
execution details just because the operator is a beginner. Explain things
clearly, but include enough detail for accurate execution. **Simple
language does not mean incomplete strategy.** A plain-English explanation
and a rigorous, detailed deliverable are not in tension — do both.

## 6. Progress standard (coordinator responsibility)

At major decision gates, the coordinator briefly tells the operator:

- **Where we are** — current state of the run.
- **What we just completed** — the deliverable just produced/approved.
- **What we are doing next** — the next step in the pipeline.
- **Why that next step matters** — how it connects to the business being
  built, not just "running the next agent."

This is the coordinator's own responsibility (not something to delegate to
a specialist agent) and applies at every quality gate, hand-off, and
approval point in `revenue-agent-runbook.md` — not only at the start and
end of a run.
