## The business, in one paragraph

A B2B AI Systems & Automation Implementation Company. The long-term beachhead is mid-market ($5M-$50M revenue) DTC e-commerce brands, selling "The Stack-Native Implementation System" — AI-driven customer support and post-purchase lifecycle automation built into a client's existing Shopify/Gorgias-or-Zendesk/Klaviyo stack, never replacing it. But the company does not start by selling to that buyer. It starts on rung 2 of a 7-rung credibility ladder: winning one first "Founding Client" — a much smaller, $1M-$5M founder-led brand — with a single, narrow, cheap-to-deliver workflow, in exchange for the proof (a testimonial and real numbers) needed to eventually sell the mature offer.

## 1. The market signal (why this beachhead, `01-market-signal-research.md`)

**Verdict:** Strong signal. Of all researched candidates, this beachhead has the clearest evidence that businesses already pay implementation-level fees specifically for AI systems, not just software — a real, evidenced gap between DIY self-serve AI tools (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin — $0.90-$1.00/resolution, no implementation help) and enterprise-only AI contracts (Decagon, ~$50K/year floor, built for much bigger companies). Nothing exists in between for a $5M-$50M brand, and nothing at all exists for a smaller $1M-$5M brand.

**Top pain points:** (1) that DIY-to-enterprise gap itself, (2) support cost scaling faster than headcount as a brand grows, (3) cart abandonment/post-purchase lifecycle neglect.

**Honest limits of the evidence:** every data point is a live vendor price or a secondary/vendor-reported figure — no independent first-person buyer complaint was found. There's a real risk that Gorgias/Intercom could close this gap themselves by adding a managed-implementation tier. E-commerce brands are unusually exposed to public backlash if AI support misfires — this shaped several later decisions below.

## 2. The offer (`02-offer-strategy.md`)

### The mature offer (the long-term target, not what's sold first)

| Tier | Price | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | $5,000-$7,500 | 1-2 support intent categories, escalation rules, basic reporting, handover docs, one training session |
| Tier 2 | $10,000-$20,000+ | Adds more categories, sentiment/VIP routing, full Klaviyo lifecycle + cart-recovery rebuild, dashboard, 30-day optimization |
| Retainer | $500-$2,000+/month | Ongoing monitoring, template updates, monthly reporting |
| Paid discovery/audit | $1,500-$3,000 | For larger/skeptical prospects; credited toward implementation if they proceed |

**Mechanism:** integrate, never replace. **Why now:** cost compounds structurally as a brand grows, plus a genuine seasonal deadline (BFCM approaching) — not manufactured urgency.

### The actual first offer — Tier 0, "Founding Client" (what's really being sold right now)

**Exact scope, itemized (added on second-pass review):**
- Included: order-status/tracking intent classification only; read-only Shopify order data for that one purpose; exception/escalation rules routing anything else (refunds, complaints, ambiguous or emotional messages) to a human with full context; QA testing across an agreed sample of real and edge-case tickets with a written pass/fail log; written handover documentation; one training session.
- Not included: any other ticket category, Klaviyo/cart-recovery work, new channels, ongoing monitoring beyond a short post-launch check-in, multi-store coverage, a custom dashboard.
- **Price:** $2,500-$4,000, phased 50% at kickoff / 50% at successful launch, 2-3 weeks.
- **Why this price, not a DIY toggle (added on second-pass review):** a founder could technically flip that switch themselves. The value being sold is that it's tested safely — against real and edge-case scenarios — before it ever touches a live customer, so a bad automated response never happens in front of a paying customer first. This is risk removal, not convenience, and it's grounded directly in the research's finding that e-commerce brands are unusually exposed to reputational damage from AI mishandling a real interaction.
- **Why this specific wedge, not the top-ranked pain (added on second-pass review):** order-status/tracking was chosen because it's fast to build, fast to demo, and low-risk for a first, unproven client relationship — not because research ranked it the most urgent pain (that's the DIY/enterprise gap, addressed by the mature Tier 1/2 offer once there's proof to sell against). Client #1's urgency is instead pitched concretely: a $1-5M brand heading into Black Friday/Cyber Monday is about to see a real, dateable spike in exactly this ticket type.

### The credibility ladder (the path from here to the mature offer)

1. **Demo** — built and tested (Section 5, below).
2. **First Client** ← *you are here* — $1-5M brand, $2,500-$4,000, single workflow.
3. **Proof** — before/after metrics, testimonial, usage rights, redacted recording.
4. **Clients 2-3** — $1-8M brands, $4,000-$6,000, scope expanding toward full Tier 1.
5. **Case study/track record** — 3+ documented, quantified implementations.
6. **Larger DTC clients** — begin targeting the $5-50M beachhead; full Tier 1 as the floor.
7. **Premium implementations** — the mature offer, sold to the buyer it was designed for, backed by real evidence.

**What stays constant at every rung:** integrate-never-replace, the human-escalation safety net, testing/documentation discipline, scope-protection against creep. Even the discounted first engagement gets full-price rigor — never a permanent cheap tier.

## 4. The conversion system (`04-conversion-system.md`)

**No CRM, no email list, no paid tools assumed.** One real gap flagged and still open: set up a free Stripe Payment Link or PayPal invoice before your first "yes" — there's currently no way to collect the phased payment.

**Lead magnet:** the demo itself — no separate PDF was built, since it would be redundant.

**No landing page** — a free one-page Google Doc proposal replaces it (scope, price, what's excluded, access needed, next step — now including a delivery-timeline contingency clause added on second-pass review).

**Scripts** cover: first-reply DM/email, 3 qualifying questions, a disqualification script, the offer-transition close, and a 3-touch manual follow-up sequence (then stop) — all now including an honest disclosure that the demo is a scripted prototype, not a live system (added on second-pass review).

**Outbound prospecting system — the highest-priority fix, entirely new (Section 9 of `04-conversion-system.md`):** runs in parallel with the content, doesn't wait for or depend on organic reach or an assumed warm network. In brief:
1. Identify candidate brands using only free public signals (visiting their storefront, checking for a Shopify/Gorgias/Zendesk/Klaviyo footprint, job postings, LinkedIn company size).
2. Find the founder/COO via the company's About page or LinkedIn.
3. Pre-filter for likely fit (store live over a year, active job postings, no enterprise-scale red flags).
4. Use only legitimate public information — no scraping that violates a platform's terms, no pretexting, no impersonation.
5. Send a short, personalized connection request and first message referencing something specific and true about the business.
6. Route anyone who responds into the exact same qualifying → demo → offer flow already built.
7. Track everything (inbound and outbound alike) in one simple spreadsheet pipeline.

Realistic pace: ~15-20 prospects researched and contacted per week, by hand — a real pipeline, not a mass campaign.

**Customer journey (updated):** Stage 0, outbound contact (parallel to content) → content builds credibility → same-day qualifying reply → demo walkthrough (the single biggest belief shift, since no other proof exists) → offer + one-pager → follow-up if needed → signed agreement + 50% kickoff → 2-3 week delivery → 50% launch payment + results collected → testimonial ask.

**After Client #1 succeeds:** only a small $150-$250/month monitoring add-on or a separate small scope-expansion project may be offered — explicitly not Tier 2, not the full retainer, nothing implying a track record that doesn't exist yet.

Confirmed in a real browser test: all three scenarios pass, zero errors. $0 cost, no accounts, no external dependencies. See `demo/README.md` for how to open, present, and record it.
