**Beachhead:** Mid-market ($5M-$50M revenue) DTC e-commerce brands — AI-driven customer support and post-purchase lifecycle automation, implemented on top of their existing Shopify/Gorgias-or-Zendesk/Klaviyo stack.

## 1. Who it's for

The Founder or Head of Customer Experience/Operations at a DTC e-commerce brand doing roughly $5M-$50M/year in revenue, running Shopify + Gorgias or Zendesk for support + Klaviyo for lifecycle marketing. Support has visibly outgrown what the founder or a small team can handle manually — they're either personally still answering tickets some days, or they have 2-5 support reps whose headcount can't keep pace with order volume growth. They've looked at (or already pay for) Gorgias AI or Intercom Fin and either haven't turned the AI features on because nobody has time to configure them properly, or turned them on and got shallow, generic results because self-serve setup wasn't enough. They've looked at enterprise AI vendors (Decagon-tier) and know instantly that's not for them — wrong budget, wrong company size, wrong sales process.

Simone's research itself flags the $5M-$50M boundary as inferred, not confirmed.

## 2. The specific problem (in buyer's language)

"We've outgrown doing support ourselves, but we're too small to get real help. Our AI tools require us to configure and manage them ourselves, and nobody here has the time or expertise to do that properly — so we're either paying for AI resolution we're not actually using well, or still buried in tickets. Meanwhile every abandoned cart and lifecycle gap is money we're not getting back, and we know it, but fixing it isn't anyone's full-time job."

Maps directly to research pain points #1 (no implementation/management layer between DIY and enterprise) and #2 (support cost scaling faster than headcount), with #3 (cart abandonment/lifecycle neglect) as the secondary, revenue-side version of the same underlying problem: they own tools that could solve this but lack the implementation layer to make them work.

## 3. The desired outcome

Not "AI support software installed" — the buyer wants: tickets resolved automatically at a materially higher rate without adding headcount; a measurable drop in cost-per-resolution; recovered revenue from cart abandonment and post-purchase lifecycle moments currently running on autopilot or not at all; and their own or their team's time freed from firefighting to focus on growth, not queue management. The after-state is "our support and lifecycle automation just works in the background, and I can see the resolution and revenue numbers proving it," not "we installed a new tool."

## 4. The offer itself

A done-for-you AI implementation and management service, delivered as a tiered engagement, built on top of the client's existing stack (Shopify + Gorgias/Zendesk + Klaviyo) rather than replacing it:

- **Tier 1 — Single-Workflow Implementation** (entry point): Configure and deploy one high-value automated workflow — most likely AI-driven support resolution (order status, returns, shipping, FAQ-type tickets) inside their existing Gorgias/Zendesk, tuned to their brand voice, policies, and edge cases, with resolution-rate and cost-per-ticket tracking built in from day one.
- **Tier 2 — Full Support + Lifecycle Build**: Tier 1 scope plus a custom cart-abandonment and post-purchase lifecycle automation layer built into their Klaviyo instance (multi-touch recovery sequences, win-back flows, proactive support-triggered lifecycle moments), plus escalation logic so AI and human agents hand off cleanly.
- **Recurring Management & Optimization Retainer** (attached to either tier): Ongoing monitoring, prompt/flow tuning, monthly resolution-rate and recovered-revenue reporting, and expansion planning as the brand adds channels, SKUs, or new automation targets.

## 5. The promise (sales page line)

"We implement and manage the AI support and lifecycle automation your team doesn't have time to configure — built into the tools you already use, at a fraction of what an enterprise AI contract costs."

## 6. The unique mechanism

**The Stack-Native Implementation System.** Not a proprietary AI model or a new platform — the mechanism is implementing and managing automation *inside the brand's existing Gorgias/Zendesk + Klaviyo + Shopify stack* rather than requiring rip-and-replace, combined with a done-for-you configuration-and-management layer neither the self-serve tools (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin) nor the enterprise vendors (Decagon) currently offer to this size of brand. The differentiation is structural: self-serve tools give you the engine with no driver; enterprise vendors give you a driver but only if you can afford their car. This offer is the driver, for the car they already own.

## 7. Positioning

Positioned explicitly in the gap the research identified: cheaper and more accessible than Decagon's ~$50K/year enterprise floor, deeper and more managed than what a brand can self-configure with Gorgias AI or Intercom Fin's self-serve resolution pricing, and AI-native/automation-focused rather than the labor-arbitrage model of BPO outsourcing firms. Explicitly NOT positioned as a replacement for their existing stack — the pitch is integration and activation of tools they already pay for, which lowers perceived switching risk and directly undercuts the objection "we already have Gorgias/Klaviyo, why do we need this."

## 8. What makes it valuable (price justification)

- **Cost avoidance vs. hiring:** a single US support hire runs $55K-$73K/year in salary alone before benefits/software (B-rated); this offer's full-build tier costs a fraction of one hire's annual cost, as a one-time implementation plus a retainer far below a salary.
- **Cost avoidance vs. enterprise AI:** meaningfully under Decagon's ~$50K/year platform floor.
- **Revenue recovered:** cart-recovery automation is described industry-wide as the highest-ROI e-commerce automation, with a 3-touch sequence recovering 10-15% of abandoned carts — directly quantifiable against the client's own abandoned-cart revenue.
- **Time/risk removed:** the buyer isn't spending their own or their team's time learning to configure AI resolution tools, and isn't risking a poorly-configured AI mishandling a customer interaction publicly (a real reputational risk the research explicitly flags for this audience).
- **Expertise they don't have in-house** and can't justify hiring for at this size.

## 9. Pricing / offer structure

- **Tier 1** (single-workflow implementation): $5,000-$7,500 one-time.
- **Tier 2** (full support + lifecycle build): $10,000-$20,000+ one-time, scaling with number of workflows/channels and complexity.
- **Recurring Management & Optimization Retainer:** $500-$2,000+/month, positioned as a natural continuation of the resolution-based pricing model this market has already normalized (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin) — can scale with ticket/resolution volume or added workflow scope over time.
- **Optional paid discovery/audit** for larger or hesitant prospects: $1,500-$3,000, credited toward Tier 1/2 if they proceed — consistent with the brief's comfort with paid discovery, and a lower-commitment entry point for a first close.

**EXTRAPOLATION FLAG (important):** every number above reflects established-provider/vendor pricing norms Simone's research found (Gorgias, Intercom, Decagon, industry hire costs). The research explicitly states there is no data on what a brand-new, unproven solo operator can actually charge or how fast a first deal closes in this space. **Recommendation:** treat the low end of Tier 1 ($5K, possibly with a founding-client discount and a case-study/testimonial trade for the first 1-2 clients) as the realistic opening price until real deals are closed — do not lead with Tier 2/retainer pricing until there's a proof point.

## 10. Why now

Structural, not manufactured: support cost and cart-abandonment losses scale directly with the brand's growth, so the pain compounds the longer it's left alone — every month of growth without an implementation layer widens the gap between ticket volume and team capacity, and leaves more abandoned-cart revenue on the table. There's also a genuine, dateable external deadline worth using honestly: as of this run (September 21, 2026), Black Friday/Cyber Monday is roughly two months out — the highest-volume, highest-cart-abandonment period of the year for DTC brands, and a legitimate reason to have support and cart-recovery automation implemented and tested before that surge rather than during it. This is a real seasonal fact, not fabricated urgency, and should be used as a factual timing argument, not a pressure tactic.

Secondary, longer-term "why now" (for the operator, not necessarily buyer-facing copy): the research flags that Gorgias/Intercom could move down-market into this exact segment over time, potentially closing the gap this offer occupies — an argument to act now while the gap is open.

Beachhead, offer name, and pricing bands unchanged from the approved strategy above — this is an operational refinement, not a new offer. **EXTRAPOLATION** flags below mark anywhere Camille made a practical delivery/tooling judgment call beyond what Simone's research or the brief confirmed, since none of this has been built or sold yet.

### 12.1 What exactly are we building — the customer-journey workflow

- Customer contacts the brand through an existing channel already inside their Gorgias/Zendesk (chat widget, email, or social DM inbox).
- The AI layer classifies intent into a defined, named list of categories — realistically: order status/tracking, standard return or refund policy questions, shipping delay, and address change pre-fulfillment (Tier 1 covers the top 1-2 of these; Tier 2 covers the full set plus damaged/defective item triage).
- For classification and resolution, the AI pulls permitted order/customer data from Shopify (order status, tracking number, order history, fulfillment status) via API, surfaced either natively in the Gorgias/Zendesk AI agent or through a connected orchestration layer.
- For in-scope, low-risk intents, the AI responds directly, resolves and closes the ticket, tags it by resolution type, and triggers a CSAT prompt.
- For exceptions — refunds above a client-defined dollar threshold, negative-sentiment or repeat-contact tickets, VIP/high-value customer flags, or anything outside the named intent list — the AI drafts a suggested response but routes the ticket to a human agent with a context summary attached (order details, prior messages, why it was flagged), rather than resolving it autonomously.
- Post-resolution, the interaction is logged back to the customer's Klaviyo profile as an event/tag (e.g., "contacted support: shipping delay"), which suppresses unrelated promotional sends temporarily, and (Tier 2 only) can trigger a relevant lifecycle flow — an apology/goodwill offer for a shipping problem, a replacement-tracking sequence for a damaged item, etc.
- Separately, on the revenue-recovery side (Tier 2), Shopify checkout abandonment triggers an enhanced Klaviyo flow with dynamic content referencing the actual abandoned cart items, and — where the platform supports it — a "finish your order" chat entry point that opens a support conversation pre-loaded with that cart's context if the customer responds.
- Every resolution, escalation, and recovered cart is logged for reporting: AI-resolved vs. human-resolved ticket counts, resolution rate, average handle time, estimated cost-per-resolution savings, cart-recovery rate and recovered revenue, and CSAT — pulled from native Gorgias/Zendesk/Klaviyo reporting into a consolidated view.

### 12.2 Exact deliverables

- Configured AI resolution logic inside the client's existing Gorgias AI Agent / Zendesk AI (or a custom orchestration build if native capability is insufficient — see 12.4), covering the named intent list agreed during workflow mapping.
- Live integration connections: Shopify → helpdesk (order data), helpdesk → Klaviyo (event/tag sync), plus any orchestration-tool connections if a custom build path is used.
- A written "automation playbook" documenting exactly what the AI can resolve autonomously, what data it can access, and every escalation trigger and threshold.
- Brand-voice response templates/macros, drafted and then reviewed and approved by the client before going live.
- Escalation queue and tagging structure built in the existing helpdesk.
- Klaviyo flow builds/enhancements for post-purchase and cart-recovery scenarios (Tier 2 only).
- A test-case log: every scenario tested, expected vs. actual behavior, pass/fail status.
- A reporting view (native-platform dashboard or a lightweight consolidated report) covering the metrics listed in 12.1.
- Written handover documentation: how the system works, how to edit templates/rules, escalation path, who to contact for changes.
- One live or recorded training walkthrough for the client's team.

### 12.3 Packages

**TIER 1 — AI Support Resolution, Single Workflow (~$5K-$7.5K)**
- Included: AI resolution for the client's highest-volume 1-2 support intent categories (typically order status/tracking plus one adjacent category), built inside their existing Gorgias/Zendesk, connected to Shopify order data, defined escalation rules to a human for everything else, tested against an agreed scenario set, one round of template revisions, basic native-platform reporting (resolution count, deflection rate), handover documentation, one training session.
- Not included: Klaviyo/cart-abandonment/lifecycle work, any channel not already live in their helpdesk, ongoing monitoring beyond a 2-week post-launch stabilization window, a custom dashboard build, multi-store/multi-brand coverage.
- Problem it solves: no implementation layer between DIY tools and enterprise AI, and support cost scaling faster than headcount (research pain points #1 and #2).
- Approximate implementation time: 3-4 weeks (dependent on client access speed — see 12.7).
- Why it's worth the price: delivers a standing, working capability — not a one-off audit or consulting hour — at a fraction of the cost of a single month of a full-time support hire's salary.

**TIER 2 — AI Support + Lifecycle Automation, Full Build (~$10K-$20K+)**
- Adds: 2-4 additional intent categories beyond Tier 1 (shipping delay, address change, damaged/defective item triage), sentiment-based and VIP/high-value-customer escalation routing, Klaviyo integration for support-triggered lifecycle flows (apology/win-back, replacement tracking), an AI-enhanced cart-abandonment recovery rebuild (dynamic content, chat handoff), a consolidated cross-platform reporting dashboard, a larger test-scenario library, and a 30-day post-launch optimization window included in the fee.
- Problem it solves: the full three-pain-point set from the research, including cart-abandonment/lifecycle revenue leakage, not just support cost.
- Approximate implementation time: 6-10 weeks, wider range due to the added Klaviyo build and testing surface.
- Why the higher price is justified: significantly more integration points and testing scope, plus a revenue-recovery component clients can justify against top-line dollars recovered, not only cost avoided.

**ONGOING MANAGEMENT RETAINER (~$500-$2K+/month)**
- Every month: monitor resolution accuracy and error/escalation patterns, update templates/macros as policy or products change, a monthly report and short review call covering resolution rate, deflection, CSAT, and (Tier 2 clients) recovered cart revenue trend, a capped allowance of minor rule/template tweaks, and flagging of clear expansion opportunities (new workflow candidates).
- Requires a separate project fee: adding a new intent/workflow category, adding a new channel, integrating a new platform, expanding to a new store/brand, or any major logic redesign.
- Retainer scales with scope: roughly $500/month for Tier-1-only clients, $1,500-$2,000+/month for Tier 2 clients with more moving parts.

**PAID DISCOVERY/AUDIT (~$1.5K-$3K)**
- When to use instead of selling implementation directly: for larger or more skeptical prospects, brands with messy/nonstandard tech stacks or multiple brands/stores, or any prospect unsure which tier fits — also the recommended default path early on, before the operator has case studies, since it reduces scoping risk on both sides.
- Client receives: a written audit of current ticket volume/categories (pulled from Gorgias/Zendesk data), a Klaviyo flow audit (current cart/lifecycle automation state), a specific recommended tier with a projected cost-savings/revenue-recovery estimate, and a phased implementation roadmap.
- Credit: yes — the full audit fee is credited toward Tier 1 or Tier 2 if the client proceeds within a defined window (e.g., 60 days); state this upfront so it reads as a low-risk yes, not a separate cost.

### 12.4 Software/tech stack

- **Client already owns:** Shopify (or equivalent), Gorgias or Zendesk, Klaviyo, and whatever email/SMS sending infrastructure is already tied to Klaviyo.
- **We need:** an AI/resolution capability — first choice is the platform's own native AI feature (Gorgias AI Agent or Zendesk AI), since it's faster to configure and keeps the client inside tools they already pay for; if native capability can't support the required logic (e.g., cross-platform Klaviyo triggers, custom sentiment/VIP routing), an orchestration layer such as Make.com, Zapier, or n8n connecting the Shopify API, an LLM API (OpenAI/Anthropic), the helpdesk API, and the Klaviyo API. Also needed: a lightweight reporting tool (native platform reporting first choice; Google Looker Studio or a Sheets-based rollup if a consolidated cross-platform view is required), a documentation tool (Notion or Google Docs) for handover materials, and a simple test-scenario tracker.
- **Tools that may create additional client cost:** Gorgias AI Agent and Zendesk AI are usage/resolution-priced add-ons on top of the client's existing plan (research showed roughly $0.90-$1.00 per resolution) — an ongoing client-side cost separate from our fee, must be disclosed clearly upfront; if the custom orchestration path is used instead, LLM API usage is a client or pass-through cost; a paid Make/Zapier/n8n subscription may be needed if orchestration goes beyond free-tier.
- **Optional depending on existing stack:** choice between n8n (lower cost, more setup work) and Make/Zapier (faster, higher cost) depending on client technical comfort; if the client already has a BI/reporting tool, use that instead of building a new dashboard.

**EXTRAPOLATION FLAG:** whether native platform AI is sufficient or a custom orchestration build is needed can only be confirmed per-client during discovery, once we see what their specific Gorgias/Zendesk plan and Klaviyo setup actually allow — this wasn't something Simone's research determined, since it only looked at published pricing, not platform capability limits.

### 12.5 Delivery process (signed agreement to launch)

1. **Kickoff & Access (Days 1-3):** collect helpdesk admin access, Shopify read access to orders/customers, Klaviyo access (Tier 2); review existing macros, return/refund policy, and brand voice guidelines.
2. **Systems & Ticket Audit (Days 3-7):** pull 60-90 days of ticket history to confirm actual top intent categories/volumes match the assumed scope; audit existing Klaviyo flows (Tier 2) to avoid duplicating or conflicting with what's already live.
3. **Workflow Mapping & Escalation Rules (Days 7-12):** document the exact decision tree — what the AI resolves autonomously, what data it can access or change, every escalation trigger — and draft response templates. Client review/approval checkpoint here; this step cannot be rushed since it's a policy/liability decision only the client can make.
4. **Build (Days 10-20, overlapping):** configure the resolution logic and templates, connect Shopify data access, build Klaviyo flows (Tier 2), set up the escalation queue and tagging.
5. **Testing (Days 18-25):** run the agreed scenario library — both "should resolve" and "should escalate" cases — log pass/fail, fix issues.
6. **Client Approval & Limited Launch (Days 24-28):** walk the client through test results and a live demonstration; launch to a limited channel or ticket subset first if the platform supports staged rollout.
7. **Full Deployment (Week 4-5):** expand to full ticket volume and all covered channels.
8. **Stabilization/Monitoring Window (2 weeks Tier 1, 30 days Tier 2, included in the project fee):** daily/weekly review of escalation accuracy and error patterns, quick fixes.
9. **Handover:** deliver documentation and training, then transition to the ongoing retainer if the client opts in, or a clean handoff with no further obligation if not.

### 12.6 AI vs. operator — division of labor

**AI/agents can assist with:** analyzing exported ticket data to confirm top intent categories and volumes during the audit step; drafting response templates and the automation playbook for the operator/client to review (never final-approve); generating the test-scenario library; drafting the workflow decision-tree documentation; assisting with orchestration build work (prompt drafting, API integration code); drafting the monthly retainer report narrative from raw metrics; flagging anomalies or unusual error/escalation patterns for the operator to review; drafting the discovery/audit report itself; researching a specific client's existing brand voice from their site/content to inform template drafts.

**Requires the operator's human judgment:** all client discovery calls and relationship management; final sign-off on what the AI is allowed to resolve autonomously vs. escalate (a trust and liability decision); reviewing and approving every AI-drafted template against brand and legal risk before it goes live; setting escalation thresholds for refunds, sentiment, and VIP handling; approving test results before go-live; handling any customer-facing incident involving the AI (the exact reputational risk the research flagged for this audience); all pricing and scope negotiation; enforcing the scope boundaries in 12.8; and the ongoing retainer relationship and renewal conversation.

### 12.7 Delivery time

- Tier 1: ~3-4 weeks, assuming the client grants platform access within the first week and approves templates/escalation rules within 2-3 business days of review.
- Tier 2: ~6-10 weeks, the wider range reflecting added Klaviyo build complexity and a larger test surface.
- Retainer: continuous, monthly cadence.
- Paid discovery/audit: ~1-2 weeks standalone.

**Biggest delay factors:** slow client access provisioning; delayed client approval of brand-voice templates or escalation policy (a decision only they can make, cannot be rushed or automated around); messy or inconsistent historical ticket/return-policy data requiring more discovery time than scoped; undocumented or tangled existing Klaviyo flows (Tier 2); the client needing to separately purchase or approve the Gorgias/Zendesk AI add-on before build can start.

### 12.8 Scope protection

- **Tier 1:** capped at exactly the 1-2 intent categories named in writing during workflow mapping, one existing channel set (no new channel onboarding), one round of template revisions, one Shopify store/brand. A new intent category, a second store/brand, a new channel, or custom development beyond configuration triggers a change order or an upsell conversation to Tier 2.
- **Tier 2:** capped at an explicitly named list of intent categories and Klaviyo flows written into the SOW (e.g., "up to 5 intent categories and up to 4 flows"), one store/brand. Additional stores/brands, categories beyond the named list, or new platforms (e.g., adding a loyalty tool) are separate project scopes.
- **Both tiers:** no custom LLM fine-tuning, no building a new helpdesk/CRM from scratch, no replacing the client's existing platforms — always integration, never replacement, consistent with the positioning against a full-stack-replacement pitch.
- **Retainer:** a capped monthly allowance of template/rule edits; reactive monitoring and optimization within existing scope only; any new integration or multi-day build, even for retainer clients, is quoted as a separate mini-project before starting.
- Any request outside the signed SOW is documented and quoted separately before work begins — this is the operating discipline, not an aspiration.

### 12.10 First-client / demo version (definition only — not built here)

**What it should do:** simulate an end-to-end conversation where a fake customer messages a chat widget asking about order status, the AI correctly identifies intent, looks up a sample order, and resolves it directly with an on-brand response; then a second sample message shows a trickier case (angry tone, or a refund above threshold) getting correctly escalated to a human-agent view with a context summary attached, rather than mishandled; and one lifecycle example showing a sample abandoned cart producing a personalized recovery message referencing the actual cart contents.

**Systems/tools needed:** a sandbox Shopify store with fictional products and orders, a trial Gorgias or Zendesk sandbox (or a simple mocked chat interface if trial access is limited), the chosen AI/orchestration layer connected to that sandbox, and a Klaviyo trial account for the cart-recovery half.

**Sample data:** entirely fictional store, customers, and orders — no real client or personal data, and the environment should be clearly labeled as a demonstration, never implied to be a live client, consistent with the brief's no-deception constraint.

**What the prospect should experience:** watching a realistic support conversation resolve correctly and instantly, then watching a harder case get correctly escalated instead of guessed at — proving the system knows its own limits — then seeing one concrete recovered-cart example with realistic dollar figures attached.

**Business outcome the demo should communicate:** this isn't a generic chatbot; it knows what it can safely resolve, hands off intelligently when it shouldn't guess, and it's already tied to recovered revenue, not just ticket deflection — directly answering the buyer's core fear (AI mishandling a customer publicly) and core question (does this actually work with the tools I already have).
