# Offer Strategy — ai-b2b-leverage-v1

**Beachhead:** Mid-market ($5M-$50M revenue) DTC e-commerce brands — AI-driven customer support and post-purchase lifecycle automation, implemented on top of their existing Shopify/Gorgias-or-Zendesk/Klaviyo stack.

**Prepared by:** Camille, Offer Strategy Director, from `01-market-signal-research.md`.

**Evidence quality note (Camille's own flag):** Simone's research is entirely vendor-pricing and secondary-source based (A/B/C rated) — there is no first-person buyer complaint, review, or interview confirming a $5M-$50M DTC brand has actually said "I want this" or would pay a specific price for it. Everywhere Camille goes beyond what the research directly stated, it's flagged below as **EXTRAPOLATION** so downstream agents and the operator don't treat it as validated fact.

---

## 1. Who it's for

The Founder or Head of Customer Experience/Operations at a DTC e-commerce brand doing roughly $5M-$50M/year in revenue, running Shopify + Gorgias or Zendesk for support + Klaviyo for lifecycle marketing. Support has visibly outgrown what the founder or a small team can handle manually — they're either personally still answering tickets some days, or they have 2-5 support reps whose headcount can't keep pace with order volume growth. They've looked at (or already pay for) Gorgias AI or Intercom Fin and either haven't turned the AI features on because nobody has time to configure them properly, or turned them on and got shallow, generic results because self-serve setup wasn't enough. They've looked at enterprise AI vendors (Decagon-tier) and know instantly that's not for them — wrong budget, wrong company size, wrong sales process.

**EXTRAPOLATION FLAG:** the $5M-$50M band, the "2-5 support reps," and the "turned Gorgias AI on but got shallow results" detail are inferred from the research's stated gap and audience description, not confirmed by a named buyer. Simone's research itself flags the $5M-$50M boundary as inferred, not confirmed.

## 2. The specific problem (in buyer's language)

"We've outgrown doing support ourselves, but we're too small to get real help. Our AI tools require us to configure and manage them ourselves, and nobody here has the time or expertise to do that properly — so we're either paying for AI resolution we're not actually using well, or still buried in tickets. Meanwhile every abandoned cart and lifecycle gap is money we're not getting back, and we know it, but fixing it isn't anyone's full-time job."

Maps directly to research pain points #1 (no implementation/management layer between DIY and enterprise) and #2 (support cost scaling faster than headcount), with #3 (cart abandonment/lifecycle neglect) as the secondary, revenue-side version of the same underlying problem: they own tools that could solve this but lack the implementation layer to make them work.

## 3. The desired outcome

Not "AI support software installed" — the buyer wants: tickets resolved automatically at a materially higher rate without adding headcount; a measurable drop in cost-per-resolution; recovered revenue from cart abandonment and post-purchase lifecycle moments currently running on autopilot or not at all; and their own or their team's time freed from firefighting to focus on growth, not queue management. The after-state is "our support and lifecycle automation just works in the background, and I can see the resolution and revenue numbers proving it," not "we installed a new tool."

## 4. The offer itself

A done-for-you AI implementation and management service, delivered as a tiered engagement, built on top of the client's existing stack (Shopify + Gorgias/Zendesk + Klaviyo) rather than replacing it:

- **Tier 1 — Single-Workflow Implementation** (entry point): Configure and deploy one high-value automated workflow — most likely AI-driven support resolution (order status, returns, shipping, FAQ-type tickets) inside their existing Gorgias/Zendesk, tuned to their brand voice, policies, and edge cases, with resolution-rate and cost-per-ticket tracking built in from day one.
- **Tier 2 — Full Support + Lifecycle Build**: Tier 1 scope plus a custom cart-abandonment and post-purchase lifecycle automation layer built into their Klaviyo instance (multi-touch recovery sequences, win-back flows, proactive support-triggered lifecycle moments), plus escalation logic so AI and human agents hand off cleanly.
- **Recurring Management & Optimization Retainer** (attached to either tier): Ongoing monitoring, prompt/flow tuning, monthly resolution-rate and recovered-revenue reporting, and expansion planning as the brand adds channels, SKUs, or new automation targets.

## 5. The promise (sales page line)

"We implement and manage the AI support and lifecycle automation your team doesn't have time to configure — built into the tools you already use, at a fraction of what an enterprise AI contract costs."

## 6. The unique mechanism

**The Stack-Native Implementation System.** Not a proprietary AI model or a new platform — the mechanism is implementing and managing automation *inside the brand's existing Gorgias/Zendesk + Klaviyo + Shopify stack* rather than requiring rip-and-replace, combined with a done-for-you configuration-and-management layer neither the self-serve tools (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin) nor the enterprise vendors (Decagon) currently offer to this size of brand. The differentiation is structural: self-serve tools give you the engine with no driver; enterprise vendors give you a driver but only if you can afford their car. This offer is the driver, for the car they already own.

## 7. Positioning

Positioned explicitly in the gap the research identified: cheaper and more accessible than Decagon's ~$50K/year enterprise floor, deeper and more managed than what a brand can self-configure with Gorgias AI or Intercom Fin's self-serve resolution pricing, and AI-native/automation-focused rather than the labor-arbitrage model of BPO outsourcing firms. Explicitly NOT positioned as a replacement for their existing stack — the pitch is integration and activation of tools they already pay for, which lowers perceived switching risk and directly undercuts the objection "we already have Gorgias/Klaviyo, why do we need this."

## 8. What makes it valuable (price justification)

- **Cost avoidance vs. hiring:** a single US support hire runs $55K-$73K/year in salary alone before benefits/software (B-rated); this offer's full-build tier costs a fraction of one hire's annual cost, as a one-time implementation plus a retainer far below a salary.
- **Cost avoidance vs. enterprise AI:** meaningfully under Decagon's ~$50K/year platform floor.
- **Revenue recovered:** cart-recovery automation is described industry-wide as the highest-ROI e-commerce automation, with a 3-touch sequence recovering 10-15% of abandoned carts — directly quantifiable against the client's own abandoned-cart revenue.
- **Time/risk removed:** the buyer isn't spending their own or their team's time learning to configure AI resolution tools, and isn't risking a poorly-configured AI mishandling a customer interaction publicly (a real reputational risk the research explicitly flags for this audience).
- **Expertise they don't have in-house** and can't justify hiring for at this size.

## 9. Pricing / offer structure

- **Tier 1** (single-workflow implementation): $5,000-$7,500 one-time.
- **Tier 2** (full support + lifecycle build): $10,000-$20,000+ one-time, scaling with number of workflows/channels and complexity.
- **Recurring Management & Optimization Retainer:** $500-$2,000+/month, positioned as a natural continuation of the resolution-based pricing model this market has already normalized (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin) — can scale with ticket/resolution volume or added workflow scope over time.
- **Optional paid discovery/audit** for larger or hesitant prospects: $1,500-$3,000, credited toward Tier 1/2 if they proceed — consistent with the brief's comfort with paid discovery, and a lower-commitment entry point for a first close.

**EXTRAPOLATION FLAG (important):** every number above reflects established-provider/vendor pricing norms Simone's research found (Gorgias, Intercom, Decagon, industry hire costs). The research explicitly states there is no data on what a brand-new, unproven solo operator can actually charge or how fast a first deal closes in this space. **Recommendation:** treat the low end of Tier 1 ($5K, possibly with a founding-client discount and a case-study/testimonial trade for the first 1-2 clients) as the realistic opening price until real deals are closed — do not lead with Tier 2/retainer pricing until there's a proof point.

## 10. Why now

Structural, not manufactured: support cost and cart-abandonment losses scale directly with the brand's growth, so the pain compounds the longer it's left alone — every month of growth without an implementation layer widens the gap between ticket volume and team capacity, and leaves more abandoned-cart revenue on the table. There's also a genuine, dateable external deadline worth using honestly: as of this run (September 21, 2026), Black Friday/Cyber Monday is roughly two months out — the highest-volume, highest-cart-abandonment period of the year for DTC brands, and a legitimate reason to have support and cart-recovery automation implemented and tested before that surge rather than during it. This is a real seasonal fact, not fabricated urgency, and should be used as a factual timing argument, not a pressure tactic.

**EXTRAPOLATION FLAG:** the BFCM timing angle is Camille's own addition based on the run date — it was not identified as a buying signal in Simone's research and should be framed as a logical, verifiable deadline, not presented as something the research found.

Secondary, longer-term "why now" (for the operator, not necessarily buyer-facing copy): the research flags that Gorgias/Intercom could move down-market into this exact segment over time, potentially closing the gap this offer occupies — an argument to act now while the gap is open.
