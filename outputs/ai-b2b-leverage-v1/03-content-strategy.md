## Primary platform: LinkedIn

### 1. Recommended content angle

There's a real, quantifiable pricing-and-capability gap between the AI support tools this exact buyer already pays for (Gorgias AI, Intercom Fin — self-serve, ~$0.90-$1.00/resolution) and enterprise AI vendors (Decagon, ~$50K/year floor) — and nobody is showing $1M-$5M founder-led DTC brands the math that proves they're stuck in that gap, not "too small" for a real fix.

### 2. Primary headline (first line of the post — the hook, visible before LinkedIn's "see more" cutoff)

"Your helpdesk already has an AI agent built in. You've probably never turned it on — and here's the actual reason why."

### 3. Backup headlines (5 — different angles)

1. **Cost-of-inaction:** "Every abandoned cart in your Shopify dashboard this week is money that already left. Here's the quiet reason it's still happening."
2. **Myth-busting:** "Gorgias AI doesn't fail 90% of the DTC brands that buy it. Nobody configures it. That's a different problem — and a fixable one."
3. **Honest seasonal urgency (real deadline, not manufactured):** "It's 9 weeks to Black Friday. If your support inbox already backs up on a normal Tuesday, here's the one thing worth fixing before then."
4. **Direct-question/empathy:** "Too big to keep answering tickets yourself. Too small for a $50K/year AI contract. Anyone else stuck exactly there?"
5. **Contrarian:** "You don't need to replace Gorgias, Zendesk, or Klaviyo. You need someone to actually turn on what you're already paying for."

### 4. Thumbnail concepts (accompanying LinkedIn image/graphic)

**Concept A — "The Gap" comparison card**
Text on image: *"DIY AI: $0.90/ticket, you configure it. Enterprise AI: $50K/yr floor. You're paying for one, priced out of the other."*
Visual: simple 3-panel flat graphic, no stock photos, no fake product screenshots. Panel 1: "Self-serve AI tools" with the $0.90-$1.00/resolution figure. Panel 2: "Enterprise AI" with the ~$50K/yr figure. Panel 3 (highlighted): "Most $1-5M brands are here" pointing at the empty middle. Clean sans-serif, two brand colors max — reads as "founder explaining the math," not "agency selling a dashboard."

**Concept B — "Napkin math" cost card**
Text on image: *"One support hire: $55K-$73K/year. One badly-handled ticket: a bad review. One workflow, done right: $2,500."*
Visual: styled like a handwritten calculation on an index card/notepad (a flat-design graphic imitating handwriting, or a real photographed notepad if the operator writes one) — three lines of "napkin math" stacked, ending on the Founding Client price. Deliberately unpolished/authentic — an over-produced dashboard mockup would undercut credibility since there's no live product to show yet.

**Flag:** do NOT use a screenshot of an actual Gorgias/Zendesk dashboard with fabricated data, and do NOT use a "results dashboard" mockup implying a live client system — that would imply proof that doesn't exist. Both concepts above are safe because they present public industry numbers, not claimed results.

### 5. Opening hook (actual first 2-3 lines, ~210 characters — what's visible before "see more")

> Your Gorgias or Zendesk plan almost certainly has an AI agent sitting inside it right now.
>
> Most $1-5M Shopify brands have never turned it on.
>
> Not because it doesn't work. Because nobody on the team has three uninterrupted weeks to configure it properly — and that's the actual gap this post is about.

### 6. Content structure (full post, beat by beat, ready to post today)

**[HOOK — 3 lines, ~35 words]** (as written in Section 5 above)

**[THE STUCK-MIDDLE PROBLEM — 3 short paragraphs, ~90 words]**
> If you're running support yourself some mornings, or you've got one person half-doing it between other jobs, you already know the two "solutions" everyone points you toward.
>
> Turn on the AI feature already inside Gorgias or Intercom — except configuring it well is a real project, and nobody on a 4-person team has the bandwidth to do it right, so it either stays off or runs shallow.
>
> Or hire real support automation — except that market starts around $50K/year, built for companies ten times your size.
>

**[THE MATH — 4 short lines, stat-driven, ~80 words]**
> Here's what it actually costs to leave this alone:
> - A single US support hire: $55,000-$73,000/year in salary alone, before benefits or software.
> - A typical e-commerce support ticket, handled manually: $2.70-$5.60. Complex ones run over $10.
> - Gorgias AI and Intercom Fin charge roughly $0.90-$1.00 per resolution — cheap, but self-serve. You still have to build and manage it.
> - Enterprise AI platforms like Decagon run around $50K/year — not built for your size or budget.
>
> That's the actual gap. Not "you're too small for AI." You're priced right out of one end and unconfigured on the other.

**[THE SECONDARY PAIN — cart/lifecycle revenue leak, 2 lines, ~45 words]**

Most brands at your size are running the default flow, not a tuned one. That's ongoing revenue, not a one-time miss.

**[THE MECHANISM — 3 lines, ~60 words]**
> What actually closes the gap isn't a new platform. It's someone implementing and managing the AI capability already inside the tools you pay for — Gorgias/Zendesk for support, Klaviyo for lifecycle — so it resolves the right tickets automatically, and hands off anything it's not confident about to a real person instead of guessing.
>
> Integrate what you have. Don't replace it.

**[HONEST FOUNDING-CLIENT PIVOT — 3 lines, ~70 words]**

>
> **[Use once the sandbox demo actually exists and runs reliably:]** I built a sandbox version of this — a fake store, real order data, a live conversation — so you can watch it resolve a normal ticket correctly, then watch it correctly hand off a harder one to a person instead of guessing at it.
>
> **[Use until the demo is built:]** I'm building the working demo for this right now — a sandbox order-status conversation that resolves correctly, and a harder case that gets handed to a human instead of guessed at, so you can see it work before anyone commits to anything.

**[TRANSITION TO OFFER — see Section 11]**

### 7. Key talking points (in order, each tied to a named pain point)

1. **The buyer is stuck in the middle, not "too small."** (Pain #1: no implementation layer between DIY self-serve and enterprise.) Reframes from "you can't afford this" to "you're priced out of one end, unconfigured on the other" — removes shame, names the real structural problem.
2. **Self-serve AI tools already exist inside their stack and are underused.** (Pain #1, config-time gap.) The most differentiating point — most content in this space sells a new platform or assumes the buyer has no tools; this buyer already owns the tool.
3. **The real cost math, in the buyer's own economic terms.** (Pain #2, support cost scaling faster than headcount.) The four named, sourced figures, framed as "here's the math nobody's shown you," never as claimed results of your own.
4. **Cart abandonment/lifecycle neglect as the secondary, revenue-side version of the same gap.** (Pain #3.) Kept brief — real but secondary; the Founding Client offer itself is support-only, not lifecycle/Klaviyo — don't oversell scope not being delivered in the first engagement.
5. **The mechanism: integrate, never replace.** Directly defuses the likely objection ("we already have Gorgias/Klaviyo, why do we need this") before it's raised.
6. **Honest new-provider framing + the safety net.** Names the newness directly rather than implying an established track record — required by both the working standard and the brief's no-fake-testimonial rule, and it's also the actual risk-reducer the buyer needs to hear: nothing resolves autonomously that the AI isn't confident about; it escalates to a person instead of guessing publicly.

### 8. Screen beats (visual/graphic elements accompanying the post)

- **At "THE MATH":** pair with Thumbnail Concept A or a simple stat-card graphic repeating the four numbers as a scannable visual.
- **At "THE MECHANISM":** optional simple two-box graphic — "Your stack: Shopify + Gorgias/Zendesk + Klaviyo" vs. "What gets added: configured AI logic + escalation rules + reporting," with an arrow — reinforces "integrate, don't replace" visually.
- **At the offer pivot:** once the sandbox demo exists, a short screen-recording (10-20 seconds, silent or narrated) of the mock conversation resolving correctly is far stronger than any static graphic — the one piece of content worth prioritizing building before this post goes live, since it's the only "proof" asset honest to use at this stage.
- **Do not use:** generic AI/robot stock imagery, fake dashboard screenshots, or any graphic implying an existing client relationship.

### 9. Credibility moments (honest ones only — no fabricated results)

- **The named, sourced industry numbers themselves** — presented explicitly as public data ("here's the math," not "here's what I did for a client"). The primary credibility mechanism available at this stage, and a legitimate one.
- **Transparent Founding Client framing** — stating plainly this is a new offer/first engagement, in exchange for case-study rights, rather than hiding it. Per the working standard, this builds trust rather than costing it.
- **The escalation-to-human safety net as a designed feature**, not a claimed result — describes how the system will be built, not something already proven in production. Frame as "here's how it's built to fail safely," not "here's what it's already prevented."
- **The sandbox demo** — the single strongest credibility asset available pre-client, but only once it's actually built and reliably runs the three scenarios. Do not reference it as available until it is.
- **Phased payment (50% kickoff / 50% at successful launch)** — worth stating plainly in the CTA/DM follow-up; a genuine risk-reducer that reads as confidence, not desperation.

### 10. What to cut

- No personal origin story up front — this buyer is time-poor and evaluating a vendor decision, not following a creator's journey.
- No basic explanation of what Gorgias, Zendesk, Klaviyo, or "AI agent" are — this audience already uses these tools daily.
- No generic "AI is changing everything" framing — seen hundreds of times from every SaaS vendor's marketing; signals shallow, not credible.
- No implied dashboard, metrics, or "results" screenshots that don't exist — even a mockup risks reading as a claimed result.
- No fake urgency beyond the real BFCM date — do not manufacture additional scarcity ("only 2 spots," countdown timers) on top of the genuine seasonal deadline.
- No corporate/agency jargon ("unlock synergies," "leverage AI to drive outcomes," "revolutionize your CX") — write plain and direct, per Camille's Section 12.9 sales explanation style.
- No claiming Tier 2/lifecycle/Klaviyo capability in this post's offer — the Founding Client offer is order-status/tracking only; the cart-abandonment math is context, not a promise of what's included.

### 11. Transition to the offer (actual lines)

> I'm taking on one Founding Client to build exactly this — the order-status and tracking workflow, live inside your existing Gorgias or Zendesk, working in 2-3 weeks. It's $2,500-$4,000, half at kickoff and half when it's live and working, not all upfront. In exchange, I get to use the real before/after numbers (with your OK) as my first case study.
>
> If that's useful before Black Friday hits, comment "STACK" or send me a DM — I'll walk you through [once the demo exists: "the sandbox demo first" / until then: "exactly how it would work for your setup"], no pitch attached.
