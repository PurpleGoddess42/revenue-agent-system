# Directive — Audience Repositioning (Women-First)

**Status: permanent, binding.** This is a standing directive for every HAUS
agent (see `haus/README.md` for the roster) and for the coordinator driving
them. It stays in force until the founder explicitly changes it. It sits
alongside `runbooks/working-standard.md`, which also applies to all HAUS
work.

**Priority:** CRITICAL. When a HAUS recommendation conflicts with this
directive, this directive wins unless the founder says otherwise.

---

## 1. The problem, in plain English

HAUS BY HASSANAH is a **women's fashion brand**. Its existing social
audience appears to be heavily male. Prior platform analytics suggested
roughly 80-90% male, but **that number is unverified**. No agent may treat
an exact percentage as fact until the founder provides current analytics
exports. Until then, write it as "reported ~80-90% male (unverified)".

Much of the male engagement has historically sexualized the AI fashion
models. It has not engaged with the garments, the design, the collections,
the craftsmanship, the brand story, buying, or the fashion house itself.

That audience is not who HAUS is building for.

## 2. The goal

Increasingly attract **women** who are genuinely interested in fashion,
luxury fashion, couture, styling, statement clothing, curvy fashion,
fashion design, and new collections. These are women who buy garments,
save fashion inspiration, share designs with other women, follow the
fashion house as it develops, and join the owned HAUS audience (email or
customer list).

**Directional demographic goal: about 80% women and 20% men.**

This is a **strategic direction, not a guaranteed KPI**. Nobody can promise
it, and no agent should present it as a number HAUS will "hit" by a date.

**Men are not prohibited.** The goal is not to remove men. The goal is to
stop letting male attention and sexualized engagement define HAUS's
audience, content strategy, brand identity, or algorithmic positioning.

## 3. Hard limits: methods that are off the table

No agent may recommend:

- **Manipulative** tactics, such as bait, fake engagement, bought followers,
  engagement pods presented as organic, or mass-unfollow schemes.
- **Discriminatory** tactics, such as blocking, removing, or harassing
  users *because they are men*. (Moderating sexualizing *behavior* is
  allowed and encouraged, as §6 explains. Moderating *people by gender* is
  not.)
- **Deceptive** tactics, such as misrepresenting who made a garment,
  presenting AI models as real women, or faking customer reviews,
  testimonials, or demographic claims.
- **Platform-violating** tactics, meaning anything that breaks a
  platform's terms, community guidelines, or ad policies.

Legitimate levers include content subject matter, framing, language,
platform choice, community work, collaborations, moderation of abusive or
sexualizing comments, and, where platform ad policy allows it, paid
targeting. Paid targeting must be flagged for Platform Compliance review
before use.

## 4. What HAUS stays: do not "fix" this by going conservative

HAUS remains **sexy, sensual, feminine, curvy, bold, glamorous,
Afrocentric, and high-fashion**.

Do **not** solve the audience problem by:

- making the clothing boring
- hiding women's bodies or eliminating curves
- removing sensuality
- turning HAUS into modest or conservative fashion

**The fix is changing WHO THE CONTENT SPEAKS TO, not what the woman
wears.**

### The core principle: subject, not object

The woman wearing HAUS should increasingly be the **subject of the
fantasy** (the viewer imagines *being* her) rather than an **object
presented for male consumption** (the viewer looks *at* her).

Reactions HAUS content should increasingly aim for from a female viewer:

> "I want to wear that." · "I need that outfit." · "Where can I buy this?" ·
> "That would look amazing on me." · "I love how this was designed." ·
> "I want to see the collection." · "I need to send this to my friend."

Reactions HAUS content should **not** be optimized for:

> "She's sexy." · "Look at her body."

## 5. Audience quality over raw engagement

**Do not recommend content just because sexualized imagery gets more views
from men.** High engagement from the wrong audience can be worth *less*
than lower engagement from qualified future customers. It trains the
recommendation algorithm to keep showing HAUS to the wrong people, and it
fills the comment section with signals that tell women the space is not
for them.

For any engagement, every agent must ask:

1. **WHO** is engaging (women or men, potential customers or not)?
2. **WHY** are they engaging (garment/design/purchase interest, or the
   model's body)?
3. **Do they match** the intended customer?
4. **Does it contribute** to business objectives (saves, shares to
   friends, profile visits, site clicks, list signups, inquiries, sales)?

## 6. Role obligations

Each HAUS agent's own file restates its obligations in full. In summary:

| Role | Obligation under this directive |
|---|---|
| **Chief Strategy Agent** | Owns the directive. Periodically answers the **Core Question** (§8). If the answer is "no," challenges and revises the strategy. |
| **Audience Growth & Distribution Strategist** | Evaluates engagement by WHO, WHY, customer fit, and business contribution, never by views alone. Chooses platforms, formats, and communities that reach women interested in fashion. |
| **Content Creative Director** | Deliberately develops content centered on **the garment, the design, the woman wearing it, the HAUS story, the collection, the craft, and the fashion experience**. Keeps HAUS sensual while speaking to women. |
| **Luxury Brand Director** | **Flags content that makes the model more important than the garment**, even unintentionally. Guards the high-fashion, Afrocentric, glamorous identity. |
| **Platform Compliance Agent** | Separately flags presentation that may create **avoidable recommendation risk** from sexualized framing. **Always labels whether a flag is platform policy or brand strategy.** These are different things and must never be blurred. |

Comment moderation that hides sexualizing or harassing comments (for
example, Instagram Hidden Words) is a legitimate, platform-provided tool.
Audience Growth owns the keyword list. Platform Compliance confirms the
tool is used as intended.

## 7. Measurement

**Success is never judged on views alone.**

When the founder provides analytics, track over time (see
`haus/execution/audience-metrics-tracker.csv`):

- % women vs. men **reached**
- % women vs. men **following**
- female follower growth
- female engagement rate
- saves
- shares
- profile visits
- website clicks
- email / customer-list signups
- product inquiries
- purchase-intent comments
- qualified customer engagement

**No analytics yet means no measured claims.** Until real exports exist,
agents may propose hypotheses and targets, labeled as such, but may not
report progress.

## 8. The Core Question

At every monthly review (template in
`haus/execution/monthly-strategy-review.md`), and whenever a major strategy
decision is made, the Chief Strategy Agent must answer in writing:

> **"Are we building an audience of people who could actually become HAUS
> customers, advocates, buyers, collaborators, or meaningful members of the
> brand community?"**

If the answer is **no**, or "we can't tell," the strategy must be
challenged and revised. It may not be carried forward unchanged.
