# HAUS Audience Repositioning Strategy — v1

**Governing directive:** `haus/directives/audience-repositioning.md`
**Status:** v1 draft for founder review. Not yet validated against HAUS
analytics, because none have been provided.

## Evidence labels used in this document

- **[SOURCED]**: backed by a cited public source (listed at the bottom).
  Re-check before relying on it, because platforms change.
- **[HYPOTHESIS]**: a reasoned recommendation not yet tested on HAUS. Each
  one must be tested and measured before it is treated as working.
- **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED]**: cannot be decided without information only
  the founder has.

---

## 0. Plain-English summary

**What we're doing:** changing who HAUS content *speaks to*, from men
looking at a model to women imagining themselves in the garment, without
making HAUS less sexy, curvy, bold, or Afrocentric.

**Why:** the current audience (reported ~80-90% male, unverified) mostly
isn't buying women's clothing. Platforms learn from who engages, so every
post that pulls in male "look at her body" engagement teaches the algorithm
to find *more* of that audience.

**What you (the founder) personally need to do first:**

1. Export current audience analytics from each platform (see §7). This is
   the single most important input. Without it, nothing here can be
   measured.
2. Approve or edit the content pillars (§2) and the framing test (§3).
3. Set up comment moderation (§5.1), which takes about 20 minutes.

**What the agents do:** draft content, captions, and hooks; review every
post against the scorecard; track the metrics; and run the monthly Core
Question review.

## 1. Diagnosis: why the audience skewed male

Each item below is a **[HYPOTHESIS]** to confirm against analytics and a
content audit of the last 30-50 posts.

1. **The body is the subject, not the garment.** Full-body AI model shots
   with no garment detail, story, or price make the *woman* the product.
2. **The male gaze in framing.** Camera angles, poses, and crops that
   present the model *to* a viewer (looking up at her, lingering body
   crops, over-the-shoulder "come here" poses) rather than showing a woman
   *in her own moment*.
3. **AI-model novelty.** "AI woman" content is its own genre with a
   predominantly male audience. If HAUS content reads as "AI girl" before it
   reads as "fashion house," it gets sorted into that genre.
4. **The comment section as a signal.** When women see a comment section
   full of men sexualizing the model, they conclude the space isn't for
   them and scroll on. This compounds over time.
5. **Algorithmic feedback loop.** Early male engagement leads the platform
   to show the content to lookalike men, which produces more male
   engagement.
6. **Captions that don't give women a job.** Captions without garment
   names, styling cues, "save for later," "send to your friend," or
   "where to buy" leave only one thing to react to: the body.

## 2. Content pillars, centered on garment and woman as subject

Target mix: roughly 80% of posts from pillars A-F. **[HYPOTHESIS]**

| # | Pillar | What it is | Why women engage |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **The Garment Up Close** | Fabric, drape, construction, hardware, fit on a curvy body, movement. Detail shots and slow pans. | Answers "how would this feel and fit on me?" and invites saves. |
| B | **Design Development** | Sketch → mood board → AI render → pattern → sample → final. "How this dress was born." | Makes HAUS a fashion *house*. Women follow a designer's journey. |
| C | **Founder / Creative Director** | Hassanah's voice: inspiration, Afrocentric references, why curves deserve couture, decisions and struggles. | Women connect with a woman building a brand. This is the strongest trust signal. |
| D | **Styling Education** | "3 ways to wear…", occasion styling, what to pair, styling for curves, accessorizing. | A direct "that would work on me" trigger that gets saved and shared. |
| E | **Collection Storytelling** | Collection themes, cultural references, names, the story behind each piece. | Builds anticipation for launches and list signups. |
| F | **Fashion Commentary** | Red-carpet and runway takes, curvy representation in luxury, Afrocentric design history, trend reactions. | Joins conversations women are already having. |
| G | **Glamour Editorial** | Full sensual, bold HAUS imagery, *framed per §3*. | Keeps HAUS sexy and aspirational, with her as the subject. |

Pillar G is not removed. It is the brand's heat. It just stops being the
*only* thing, and it gets framed differently.

## 3. The framing test: subject vs. object

Run every visual through these checks. This is the heart of the Luxury
Brand Director's review (scorecard in
`haus/execution/content-review-scorecard.md`).

| Leans OBJECT (for male viewer) | Leans SUBJECT (for female viewer) |
|---|---|
| Crop cuts off the head or face; frame centers the body part | Full look visible; face and expression present; the garment is readable |
| Pose performs *for* the camera ("come here") | Pose shows her *in her moment*: walking, arriving, laughing, owning the room |
| Camera looks up at or lingers on the body | Camera at eye level or with her; the garment moves with her |
| No context: plain background, just her | Context women want: event, city, gala, dinner, her own glamour |
| Caption about her ("she's a vibe 🔥") | Caption about the garment and the wearer ("The Adaeze gown, cut for curves…") |
| Viewer role: look at her | Viewer role: **be** her, save it, send it, buy it |

**The one-line test:** *Would a woman who loves fashion watch this and
think about wearing it, or only a man watching and thinking about her?*

Sensuality stays. A plunging neckline, a thigh-high slit, a sculpted
corset, curves on display: all fine. The difference is **whose fantasy the
frame serves**.

## 4. Hooks, captions, and CTAs

### Hooks (first 1-2 seconds / first line), written to the woman viewer

- "This is what couture for curves should look like."
- "POV: you walk into the gala in this."
- "The corset took 4 tries to get right. Here's why."
- "Tell me which one you're wearing: 1, 2, or 3?"
- "Designing the dress I couldn't find anywhere."
- "How to style a statement gown without it wearing you."
- "Send this to the friend who's getting married."

Avoid hooks that point at the model's body ("wait for it…", "she's
different", "rate her").

### Caption structure [HYPOTHESIS]

1. **Garment name + one sensory detail** ("The Nala dress: liquid satin
   that holds every curve.")
2. **Design or story line** (inspiration, craft, Afrocentric reference)
3. **Styling cue or occasion**
4. **One CTA** (below)

### CTAs that build the right audience

- "Save this for your next event."
- "Send this to the friend you'd wear it with."
- "Which colorway: burgundy or gold?" (comments about *the garment*)
- "Join the HAUS list for first access to the collection." (owned audience)
- "Link in bio to see the full collection."

Each CTA asks for a behavior that fashion-interested women do far more
than drive-by viewers do (saving, sending to friends, joining a list).
That shifts which engagement the algorithm rewards. **[HYPOTHESIS, test
it]**

## 5. Distribution and community

### 5.1 Comment moderation (do this first)

- Turn on **Instagram Hidden Words** with a custom list of sexualizing
  terms, emojis, and phrases (Instagram allows up to 1,000). Hidden
  comments stay visible to the person who wrote them but not to anyone
  else. **[SOURCED]** Use equivalent filters on TikTok, Facebook, and
  YouTube.
- Pin garment-focused and purchase-intent comments to the top.
- Reply warmly to women's comments about the garment, sizing, or occasions.
  Visible brand replies signal who the space is for.
- This moderates **behavior, not gender**. A man asking "does this come in
  plus sizes for my wife?" is welcome.

### 5.2 Platform selection

| Platform | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Pinterest** | **Priority add.** Fashion inspiration, saves, and traffic to the site. | Audience is roughly 70% women globally, and women 25-34 are its largest segment. **[SOURCED]** Save-based discovery also fits garment-first content. |
| **Instagram** | Core brand home. Editorial and Reels. | Needs reframing per §3 and moderation per §5.1. |
| **TikTok** | Design-development, founder, and styling content. | Treat as its own voice-led channel, not a repost of editorial shots. [HYPOTHESIS] |
| **Email / customer list** | **The owned audience.** Every platform should funnel here. | Platform demographics can drift; the list can't be taken away. |
| **YouTube** | Later: collection films and design documentaries. | [HYPOTHESIS] Lower priority until content capacity exists. |

### 5.3 Female fashion communities and collaborations [HYPOTHESIS]

- Engage genuinely (comment, share, feature) with curvy fashion creators,
  Black and African women's fashion communities, bridal and occasion-wear
  communities, and fashion-design students and educators.
- **Collaborations:** curvy and plus-size stylists and fashion creators
  (women) doing "styling the HAUS piece" content; Afrocentric designers,
  jewelers, and headwrap makers; women photographers and illustrators.
- **Real women wearing HAUS** (once garments exist physically) is the
  strongest subject-not-object signal there is. Plan for it as soon as
  samples are available. **[FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: production status]**

### 5.4 Paid distribution (optional, later)

Paid social can target by gender and interest for fashion products. Special
ad categories restrict targeting for housing, employment, credit, and
similar areas, not for apparel. **Platform Compliance must confirm current
ad policy before any spend.** Paid is a supplement, never a substitute for
fixing the content itself.

## 6. AI models: transparency and recommendation risk

Recommendation eligibility and brand strategy are separate questions, and
the Platform Compliance Agent keeps them separate.

- **Platform policy [SOURCED]:** Instagram may show sexually suggestive
  content (including see-through clothing) to followers but make it less
  likely to be recommended to non-followers in Explore, Reels, and hashtag
  search. For teen accounts it applies stricter limits. Sexualized framing
  can therefore quietly cut reach to *new* audiences, including the women
  HAUS wants to reach.
- **Brand strategy (not policy):** content can be fully within policy and
  still be off-strategy under the directive. Flag the two separately.
- **AI disclosure:** follow each platform's current AI-content labeling
  rules. Being open that images are AI-rendered design visuals also helps
  position HAUS as a design house using AI, not an "AI girl" account.
  **[HYPOTHESIS]** Platform Compliance must verify current labeling rules.

## 7. Measurement plan

**Step 1: baseline (founder action).** Export for each platform:

- Instagram professional dashboard: audience gender split for **followers**
  and **accounts reached**, top posts, saves, shares, profile visits, and
  link taps.
- The TikTok, Pinterest, and Facebook equivalents.
- Email list size, if any, and website traffic and clicks.

Log it in `haus/execution/audience-metrics-tracker.csv` as the baseline row.

**Step 2: weekly logging.** Log the same fields weekly.

**Step 3: per-post tagging.** Tag each post by pillar (A-G) and scorecard
result so the team can see which pillars move the % of reach that is women
and which drive saves and signups.

**Leading indicators to watch first** [HYPOTHESIS]: % women among accounts
**reached** moves before % women among **followers**, and the
saves-to-views and shares-to-views ratios move before sales. Follower
composition will shift slowly because the existing male followers remain.
That is expected, and it is not failure.

**Do not judge by views.** A post with fewer views but a higher share of
women reached and more saves beats a viral post from the wrong audience.

## 8. Now / Next / Mature

| Stage | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Now** | No verified analytics. Reported male-skewed audience. AI-model imagery. Comment sections likely sexualized. |
| **Next (first ~30 days)** | Baseline exported. Hidden Words on. Every post passes the scorecard. Pillars A-F make up most posts. Pinterest account live. Email capture ("join the HAUS list") set up with somewhere to land. |
| **~90 days** | First monthly trend in % women reached. Pillars ranked by female reach and saves. First women-creator collaborations. Core Question answered with data. |
| **Mature** | Women-majority reach and growing women-majority following. Owned list of future customers. Real women wearing HAUS. Collection launches driven by the list, not the algorithm. |

**Named dependencies (bridges needed):**

- **Analytics exports.** Only the founder can supply these.
- **A website or landing page with email capture.** Needed for "join the
  HAUS list." [FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED: does one exist?]
- **Physical garments or samples.** Needed for real-wear content and
  sales. [FOUNDER INPUT NEEDED]
- **Founder on-camera or voice comfort.** Pillar C is strongest with the
  founder's own voice. It can start as voiceover or text if needed.

## 9. Risks

- **Short-term view drop.** Moving away from male-bait framing may lower
  raw views at first. That is expected and acceptable under directive §5.
  Judge by the % of reach that is women and by saves, not by views.
- **Over-correction.** Going conservative would violate directive §4. The
  scorecard checks both directions.
- **AI-model stigma among women.** Some women may distrust AI-only
  imagery. Mitigate with design-process transparency and real-wear content
  when possible.
- **Unverified baseline.** Until analytics arrive, the 80-90% figure could
  be wrong in either direction.
